This is the difference

Static tracks play the same thing.
A band responds.

Other tools play a fixed backing track on loop. Jam-a-Lot's band is generated on the fly, bar by bar — which means you can shape the performance while you play. This is what practicing with a real rhythm section feels like.

01

Live dynamics, chorus to chorus

Change the shape of the next chorus without stopping. Drop to a sparse two-feel for the bass solo, double time into the out head, push the drummer to full intensity for the climax. One tap, takes effect at the next phrase boundary.

  • Next chorus: double time / half time / drop section
  • Drummer intensity: 1–5 density profiles, live
  • Solo order: head → trade → out, planned or improvised
02

Trade bars with the drummer

Press trade and the band hands off — fours, eights, or choruses — aligned to phrase boundaries. You play your line, the drummer answers. It's the single best way to build vocabulary under pressure, and it's the thing a backing track can never do.

  • Trade 4s / 8s / choruses, phrase-locked
  • Drummer solos with comping behind you, and vice versa
  • Seamless handoff kicks and splash at the swap
03

Living changes — reharmonize on the fly

Turn on Living changes and the harmony evolves as you play: common substitutions, tritone swaps, back-door turns, turnaround variants — applied tastefully over recognized forms (blues, rhythm changes, minor blues, and more). Same chart, fresh changes every chorus. Off by a tap; never written to your tune.

  • Blues, minor blues, rhythm changes, turnarounds
  • Substitutions show in the chart and the bass line, in real time
  • 100% runtime — your stored chart is never mutated
04

Feels, transposition, loops — instantly

Switch feel mid-tune (swing to bossa for the bridge), transpose ±1 semitone without re-clicking, loop a section until it's under your fingers. Everything is computed from absolute beat positions, so changes land clean with no drift, no glitch.

  • Twelve feels: swing, ballad, bossa, latin, waltz, soul-jazz, gypsy jazz, funk, blues shuffle, R&B, rock, EDM
  • Per-chorus feel plan: swing the head, bossa the solos, swing the out
  • Section loop with correct repeated-section resolution
  • Transpose in semitones; key updates and auto-saves
And this is the new part

Turn on your mic.
The band starts listening.

Open the Listening panel and Jam-a-Lot hears you play. It subtracts its own sound from what comes back through the mic — per frequency band, not with a crude gate — so what's left is you. Then the band responds to it, in real time.

01

It follows your energy

Play softer and the band comes down with you — sparser drums, thinner comping. Build and it builds. The response time is yours to set: half a chorus by default, so it reacts like a player rather than a compressor.

  • Intensity, density and register meters, live
  • Speaker calibration for playing without headphones
  • Every response strength is a slider — or off
02

It answers your phrases

Leave a gap at the end of a line and the band fills it. Question and answer, the way a rhythm section actually converses with a soloist — not a fill on a timer.

  • Phrase-gap detection from your onsets, not the clock
  • The piano's voicing register follows how high you're playing
  • Your comping density drives the piano's
03

It catches a figure you repeat

Play the same rhythmic figure twice and the drummer locks onto it, plays it with you with growing commitment through the rest of the section, and hands it back into the fill at the section end. The panel shows you when a figure is locked.

  • Quantized on the feel's own grid — swung eighths stay swung
  • Locks on one-bar or two-bar figures
  • Resolves into the section fill, never on top of it
04

It tells you where you are

You shouldn't have to count bars. A section change is announced twice — a fill into it and a cymbal on its downbeat — and a new chorus is deliberately louder than a new section, so "we went around" and "we hit the bridge" don't sound alike.

  • Every drum trade lands on the "and of 4" before the band returns
  • The whole band states the re-entry downbeat together
  • Latin styles mark arrivals with a timbale, not a borrowed kick
Everything else

A complete practice studio.

Beyond the live controls, everything you need to woodshed a tune — in one tab.

🎼

Editable chord chart

In-place editing with a root × quality × extension picker, section and form control, bar-count transforms, numbered endings, and a roadmap strip. Or paste a whole chart as text and let it parse.

🎸

Generated rhythm section

Walking bass, feel-aware drums, and comping piano — derived deterministically from the chart with seeded humanization that actually grooves. The piano comps from a real vocabulary of cells, not a loop.

🎛️

Live mixer with a stage plot

Levels in real decibels, per-voice mutes, and a Pan column that puts each player somewhere on the stage — piano left, bass right, hats and ride where a drummer sits. The reverb follows the position.

🥁

Three kits, and a sound designer

An acoustic jazz kit, a Latin percussion section, and a groovebox synth you can shape yourself — kick tune and punch, snare snap, hat tone. Levels are remembered per kit.

🎚️

Rhythm Style Lab

Design and audition your own accompaniment styles — drum voices, comping triggers, per-beat grids, per-event inspector. Saved locally, never to the cloud.

🎹

Pick your instruments

Steinway, Rhodes or drawbar organ; upright or electric bass. Every sound is loudness-matched to the others, so switching timbre never changes the balance you dialled.

🎼

Live notation

The generated bass line renders as a real lead sheet you can read, print, or export to MusicXML. Stays put after Stop for review.

🎭

Bandstand visualizer

An animated rhythm section — drummer and bassist move with the music, derived from the actual scheduled events. Full-screen theater mode for the stage or the chart.

✍️

Write your own

Start from a blues, rhythm-changes, AABA or modal sketch, get per-bar reharm suggestions, add rehearsal marks, repeats and band punch figures. Lyrics and melody, for your own compositions only.

📚

Built-in knowledge base

An in-app wiki on the rhythm styles and what each one is doing — read the bossa clave or the la pompe while you're auditioning it.

🎧

Reference listening

Save Spotify and YouTube links to any tune for context. Provider metadata never touches your chart data.

☁️

Cloud sync

Your custom charts, arrangements and preferences follow you between browsers and devices. Beta members only — guests keep everything in the one browser.

How it works

Three clicks to a chorus.

Nothing to install, nothing to download. Open it in a tab and play.

  1. 1

    Pick a tune

    Three demo tunes are open to everyone; the other 1,379 open with a free Beta account. The chart renders instantly with section colors.

  2. 2

    Set tempo & feel

    Transpose, dial a tempo, pick a feel, shape the drum mix. Living changes are on by default.

  3. 3

    Play — then drive the band

    Hit play, count in, jam. When the chorus ends, change the dynamics, trade bars, or swap feel. It follows.
